Business Knowledge Guide

This guide shows the recommended structure for business knowledge. Each topic below becomes a topic + content pair in the configuration. Copy the template, adapt it, and paste into the topics above.

Recommended Structure

# Business Knowledge for [Your Business Name]

## 1. Identity & Offering
- What we sell (core products/services)
- Who we serve (ideal customer profile)
- What makes us different (USP)

## 2. Sales Philosophy & Approach
- How we sell (consultative, educational, relationship-first)
- What we NEVER do (push, oversell, mislead)
- Core values in sales conversations

## 3. Product/Service Deep Dive
- Each offering: what it is, who it's for, key benefits
- Common misconceptions customers have
- What "good fit" vs "bad fit" looks like

## 4. Pricing Philosophy
- How we price (value-based, project-based, retainer, etc.)
- Typical ranges or "starts at" figures
- When exact quotes are possible vs when discovery needed
- Payment terms, deposits, refunds

## 5. Objection Handling Playbook
- Top 5-10 objections we hear
- Our genuine response to each (principles, not scripts)
- When to push back vs when to accommodate

## 6. Customer Journey & Timeline
- Typical sales cycle length
- Key milestones (discovery → proposal → decision → delivery)
- What happens after they say yes

## 7. FAQ & Knowledge Base
- Technical/implementation questions
- Scope boundaries (what's included/excluded)
- Integration/compatibility info

## 8. Team & Escalation
- Who does what (for handoff context)
- When to escalate to human
- Contact info for specialist questions

Why This Structure Works

  • Markdown headers help the LLM parse and retrieve relevant sections
  • Explicit "NEVER do" lists are more effective guardrails than "do" lists
  • Principles over scripts let the agent adapt naturally to each conversation
  • Pricing philosophy replaces rigid ranges — works for custom-quote businesses
  • Escalation triggers tell the agent exactly when to hand off

Tips for Good Content

  • Write as if briefing a smart new hire — specific, not generic
  • Include real phrases your team uses ("We usually say…")
  • Document edge cases you've actually encountered
  • Keep each topic focused; split if it gets long
  • Update after every few conversations — treat it as living docs
